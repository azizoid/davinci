# Editing Rules

## Editorial Priorities

Apply these priorities in order:

1. Preserve the speaker's meaning, factual accuracy, qualifications, and intended tone.
2. Preserve the strongest complete performance.
3. Improve clarity and pacing.
4. Hide technically necessary cuts without distracting from the content.
5. Add visual variety only when it supports the message.

When two rules conflict, the higher priority wins. A slightly slower truthful cut is better than a fast misleading one.

## Dialogue Cleanup

### Filler Words And Hesitations

- Remove isolated fillers such as "um", "uh", "erm", "hmm", and other non-semantic hesitation sounds when the removal is inaudible and does not damage cadence.
- Keep discourse markers such as "well", "so", "like", or "you know" when they carry meaning, tone, contrast, or conversational character.
- Do not create clipped breaths, broken consonants, room-tone holes, or unnatural word collisions to remove a filler.
- Prefer a clean alternate take, a room-tone-supported audio edit, or a visually covered cut over an audible micro-cut.

### False Starts And Mistakes

- Remove abandoned sentence openings when a complete restart follows.
- Preserve setup words needed to understand the restart.
- Remove spoken corrections only when the corrected version is clear and complete.
- Never splice words into a new claim the speaker did not make.

### Repetitions

- Remove accidental repeated words and duplicate attempts.
- When the same idea appears in several takes, keep the version that is most complete, concise, confident, natural, technically clean, and continuous with surrounding material.
- Keep intentional rhetorical repetition.

### Pauses And Breaths

- Shorten pauses that are clearly caused by searching for words, checking notes, restarting, or production delay.
- Preserve pauses needed for sentence boundaries, emphasis, emotion, comprehension, or visual transitions.
- Do not use one fixed silence threshold for all speakers. Establish a baseline from the recording and judge outliers relative to that cadence.
- Preserve natural breaths unless they are unusually loud, duplicated by an edit, or create a distracting gap.

## Best-Take Selection

Rank candidate takes using:

1. Semantic completeness and correctness.
2. Alignment with surrounding context.
3. Confident, natural delivery.
4. Audio and image quality.
5. Ease of creating a continuous edit.
6. Concision.

Do not build a sentence from many fragments merely to maximize brevity. If candidates express materially different claims, keep the contextually supported take or flag the ambiguity.

## Pacing

- Establish a cadence appropriate to the speaker and content before making fine cuts.
- Vary pacing intentionally: explanations need processing room; transitions and familiar setup can move faster.
- Avoid relentless speech with no breathing room.
- Review cuts across full thoughts, not only at waveform or word level.
- Use room tone and short audio transitions where necessary to prevent clicks or abrupt ambience changes.

## Visual Continuity

- Use a direct cut when continuity is acceptable.
- Use punch-ins or reframing to cover visible jump cuts when source resolution supports it.
- Keep scale changes purposeful and large enough to read as intentional, while avoiding visibly degraded crops.
- Alternate framing sparingly; do not punch in on a fixed timer.
- Maintain eye-line, screen direction, and consistent headroom.
- Do not hide a bad content edit with visual effects. Fix the content edit first.

## B-Roll And Cutaways

- Add B-roll when it clarifies a concrete subject, demonstrates an action, establishes context, or covers a necessary cut.
- Select footage for semantic relevance, visual quality, tone, aspect-ratio compatibility, and credible context.
- Avoid literal footage for abstract words when it feels generic or misleading.
- Do not imply that stock footage depicts the actual speaker, event, customer, product, or location unless it does.
- Avoid repetitive stock montages, excessive transitions, and clips that compete with important dialogue.
- Record source and licensing evidence for every external asset.

## Audio Treatment

- Use the least processing needed for clear, consistent speech.
- Apply corrective processing before enhancement: repair, noise control, equalization, dynamics, then final loudness control.
- Compare processed audio against the source to detect pumping, metallic noise reduction, lisping, clipped transients, or lost room character.
- Smooth edits with appropriate fades and room tone.
- Duck permitted music under speech; do not use music to mask poor dialogue edits.

## Captions

- Caption the spoken meaning faithfully, correcting obvious recognition errors but not rewriting the speaker.
- Use punctuation and casing appropriate to the delivery language.
- Break lines at natural phrase boundaries and keep display duration readable.
- Avoid captions that obscure faces or essential on-screen information.
- Use the final timeline as the timing authority.

## Quality-Control Passes

The autonomous review MUST include:

- a content pass for meaning, omitted context, and take selection;
- an edit pass for cadence, clicks, flash frames, and jump-cut continuity;
- a visual pass for framing, B-roll relevance, and caption placement;
- an audio pass for intelligibility, artifacts, peaks, and target loudness;
- a delivery pass for duration, codec, resolution, synchronization, and file integrity.

If confidence is low, preserve the source content and include a warning in the edit report. The system MUST NOT conceal uncertainty by inventing a correction.
